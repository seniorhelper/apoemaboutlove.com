Love poems for her

The Way You Read

You read with one foot tucked beneath you and a pencil you never use, lips moving on the hard words like you're tasting them first. I have watched sunsets on three coasts. None of them held still for me the way you do when a sentence finally gives you what it promised. Turn the page. I'll wait. I have been waiting my whole life for something worth waiting this quietly for, and here you are, on chapter nine.
No. 01

Love poems for her

Inventory

Your laugh: one, slightly too loud in restaurants. Your coat: green, the button I keep meaning to sew. Your handwriting on the grocery list: tilted, hopeful, "eggs" spelled with three g's once, and I kept it. Your silence on the drive home: never empty. Your hand finding mine at the crosswalk like it's checking the stove — still there? still there. Yes. Every time. Still here.
No. 02

Love poems for her

She Plants Things

She plants things she won't see bloom for a year, tomatoes in April, bulbs in the cold, talking to them softly like they're shy. I didn't know I was soil until she knelt down next to me and started, very patiently, to believe.

Love poems for her

Saturday, 7 a.m.

The house is still asleep but you are not — I hear the kettle start its slow complaint, your bare feet on the floorboards, the one that creaks right where you always step, as if you like it. You'll bring two mugs. You'll set mine down too close to the edge, like always. And I'll pretend to sleep a minute more just to feel you lean across and say my name.
No. 04

Love poems for her

If They Ask Me

If they ask me how I knew, I'll tell them it wasn't fireworks. It was the night the power went out and you lit every candle in the drawer, even the birthday ones, and said, "Well, now it's a party." I knew right then: whatever goes dark, you're the one who finds the matches.
No. 05

Love poems for him

Your Hands

Your hands know how to fix a hinge, how to coax a stubborn jar, how to hold a sleeping child like the whole world fits in the crook of one arm. They are scarred in places I've asked about and you've only half explained. I don't need the stories. I know what they do when they find my back in a crowded room: they say, I'm here. We can leave whenever you want.
No. 06

Love poems for him

The Quiet One

You don't say much at parties. You find the dog, or the kid with no one to talk to, or the dishes no one else will touch. People ask me what you're like. I say: he's the kind of man who warms up the car for you and never mentions that he did it. I say: he's the reason I stopped being afraid of winter.
No. 07

Love poems for him

Steady

Some men are thunder. You're more like the ground after — the part that holds, the part that drinks it in and turns it green. I used to think I wanted lightning. Then I spent one ordinary Tuesday with you and understood what I'd been missing was something to stand on.
No. 08

Love poems for him

The Jacket

You gave me your jacket on our second date and I never really gave it back. It hangs on my side of the closet now, three apartments later, still smelling faintly of cedar and rain and you. Some days I wear it to the mailbox. Some days I just open the closet to make sure it's there. That's what you've become, I think — the thing I check on to know that I'm home.
No. 09

Love poems for him

What I Mean When I Say Drive Safe

I mean come back. I mean I memorized the sound of your truck in the driveway and my whole body listens for it. I mean I'll leave the porch light on even though you have a key. I mean, in the short way we've learned to say long things, I love you. Come back to me.
No. 10

Anniversary & growing old together

Twenty Years of Toast

You burn the toast and I scrape it over the sink. Twenty years of that small, black snowfall. Twenty years of you saying "it's fine, I like it crunchy" and me knowing you don't, and loving you for lying. People talk about the big days — the wedding, the house, the hospital hallway. But I think the marriage lives here, in the scraping, in the crumbs, in two people who keep choosing the same burnt morning on purpose.
No. 11

Anniversary & growing old together

The Map

Remember when we didn't know the way to anything? We argued over paper maps in the dark, turned around in towns we couldn't pronounce, ate gas-station dinners on the hood of the car. We know the way now. To the pharmacy, the school, your mother's house, the good taco place. But tonight let's take the long road home. Let's get a little lost on purpose. I still like the version of us that didn't know where we were going, only that we were going there together.
No. 12

Anniversary & growing old together

Silver

Your hair has gone silver at the temples and you keep apologizing for it, as if I didn't marry the whole road, as if I'd want a love that never weathered. Every gray strand is a winter we got through. Every line by your eyes is a joke I told. Darling, look at us — we've become the old couple on the bench we used to point at and say, "someday, maybe, if we're lucky." We were lucky.
No. 13

Anniversary & growing old together

Still

Still the first one I want to tell. Still the one who knows how I take my coffee and what I mean when I say "I'm fine." Still the hand under the table at long dinners. Still the reason I hurry home. Of all the words I've given you over the years, this one might be the truest: still.
No. 14

Anniversary & growing old together

Fifty Years, a Kitchen Table

This table has held everything: bills and birthday cakes, homework, bad news, a wedding dress being hemmed, your reading glasses, my reading glasses, two cups of coffee every single morning for fifty years. The wood is scarred where the kids carved their names. We never sanded it. We never will. Some things should show how much they've been loved.
No. 15

Wedding & vows

Vow

I don't promise you a life without rain. I promise an umbrella, and if I forget it, I promise to get wet beside you and laugh about it. I don't promise I'll always be easy. I promise I'll always come back to the table. I promise to learn you like a language I'll be fluent in when we're old and still be finding new words for. Here's my hand. It's yours. Not for a season. For the whole long weather of us.
No. 16

Wedding & vows

The Morning Of

Somewhere in this city, right now, you're getting dressed for me, and I'm getting dressed for you, and neither of us can tie anything properly. Our mothers are crying already. Someone has lost the rings twice. The flowers are perfect and slightly wilting. None of it matters. In an hour I will look down an aisle full of everyone we love and see only you, walking toward the rest of my life like it's the easiest thing.
No. 17

Wedding & vows

Two Rivers

Two rivers meet below the bridge in town — one clear and cold from the mountain, one brown and warm from the plains. For a mile you can see them side by side, not mixing, just traveling together. Then a bend, a rush of stones, and they become one water. I don't want to lose myself in you. I want that mile where we run side by side, and then the bend, and then whatever we become together.
No. 18

Wedding & vows

A Blessing for the Two of You

May your arguments be short and your dinners long. May you always have one more blanket than you need. May the porch light be on when either of you comes home late. May you grow a little more like each other every year, but never so alike you stop surprising each other. May your house be loud with the people you love, and quiet when you need it to be. And when you're old, may you still reach, without looking, for the other one's hand.
No. 19

Wedding & vows

What the Ring Says

It's a small thing to carry, lighter than a key. But it says: someone is waiting. It says: I chose, and was chosen. It says, every time I wash my hands or grip a steering wheel or wave hello — I belong to a promise and the promise belongs to me.
No. 20

Long distance

Time Zones

When it's morning where you are, it's the middle of my night, so I fall asleep holding your sunrise and wake up holding your dark. We've learned to love in the gaps — voice notes, blurry photos of your coffee, "goodnight" sent at noon. The miles are real. So is this. I've never worked this hard to be close to anyone.
No. 21

Long distance

Your Sweatshirt

It stopped smelling like you on day eleven. I wear it anyway. I wear it to sleep and to the grocery store and on video calls, so you can see I'm keeping something of yours warm.
No. 22

Long distance

The Same Moon

I know it's the oldest trick in the book, looking up and thinking you're looking up too. I know the poets used it first. I don't care. Tonight it's hanging low and orange over the parking lot and I'm standing by my car like a fool, and somewhere a thousand miles east, I'd bet my whole heart you are too.
No. 23

Long distance

Countdown

Forty-one days. I've been crossing them off a calendar like a kid waiting for summer. Forty. Thirty-nine. I've already planned the airport: I'll stand at the bottom of the escalator holding a ridiculous sign, and when I see you I'm going to forget every clever thing I meant to say and just hold on.
No. 24

Long distance

Postcard

Wish you were here, it says on the front, under a picture of a beach I've never seen. On the back I wrote nothing about the weather. I wrote: the coffee here is terrible. I wrote: I keep turning to show you things. I wrote: here isn't anywhere without you in it.
No. 25

Falling in love

The First Time You Said My Name

There are a hundred people who say my name. Teachers, cousins, the man at the hardware store. I've heard it my whole life and never noticed it. Then you said it, once, on the steps outside the library, like you were turning it over in your hand, like you'd found something worth keeping — and I've been listening for it ever since.
No. 26

Falling in love

Falling

Nobody tells you falling is the easy part. You don't decide. You don't plan. One day you're just a person crossing a room, and the next, the whole room has rearranged itself around one laugh. It's the staying that takes courage. Waking up and choosing, again, to keep falling, softly, on purpose.
No. 27

Falling in love

Nervous

I changed my shirt three times. I practiced what I'd say in the car. I forgot every word of it the second you opened the door and said, "Hey, you made it," like you'd been hoping I would.
No. 28

Falling in love

The Text

It just said, "home safe?" Two words. A question mark. But I read it four times in the hallway with my shoes still on, because nobody had asked me that in years, and it felt like someone had left a light on in a house I didn't know was mine.
No. 29

Falling in love

Before

Before you, I thought love was loud. I thought it was a song on the radio, a scene in the rain, a grand speech. Now I know it's quieter than that. It's you, saving me the last bite. It's you, remembering I hate cilantro. It's me, realizing I already know how you take your coffee, and that I want to know it for years.

Sonnet, haiku & classic forms

Sonnet for an Ordinary Love

They'll never write a ballad about us, no duels, no ships, no letters sealed in wax; our greatest drama is a missed bus and who forgot to take out all the sacks. And yet I'd trade no epic for these days — your humming while you hunt for both your shoes, the way you warm your hands around a glaze of coffee cups, the way you read the news out loud to me in voices, badly done. The poets wanted lightning, I want this: the long, unwritten hours, one by one, the lazy, unremarkable good-night kiss. Let others chase a love the world can see. I'll take the quiet one you've given me.
No. 36

Sonnet, haiku & classic forms

Four Haiku for Four Seasons

Spring: your muddy boots lined up beside mine. Two pairs walking the same paths. Summer: one fan, one sticky night, still you reach for my hand in your sleep. Autumn: you rake leaves into a heart on the lawn. The wind keeps the heart. Winter: frost on glass. You write our two initials. They outlast the snow.
No. 37

Sonnet, haiku & classic forms

Aubade

Don't open the curtains yet. The sun can wait. It's been rising over this street for a hundred years; one more minute won't hurt its reputation. Stay here, in the warm shape of the blankets, in the half-light that hasn't decided on a color, while the city starts its engines and its arguments. Out there they want you back — the inbox, the traffic. In here it's still the night we didn't sleep, still your heartbeat under my ear like a clock that only keeps one kind of time. Five more minutes. Then I'll let the morning have you.

Acrostic: L.O.V.E.

Late nights talking until the birds start up, Old jokes that still make you snort your drink, Vacuuming the whole house before my mother visits, Every small thing, done for me, without me asking.

Sonnet, haiku & classic forms

Villanelle of the Porch Light

I leave the porch light on when you're away. It doesn't bring you home a minute sooner. I leave it on because I always say the house remembers you. Throughout the day the empty rooms keep listening, like a tuner. I leave the porch light on when you're away, and moths come by to circle it and stay, and neighbors think I'm careless or a crooner. I leave it on because I always say the dark should know whose house this is. Okay — call it a habit, call me a lovesick schooner. I leave the porch light on when you're away. And when your headlights turn into the bay and sweep the yard, I'm out the door, no sooner. I leave it on because I always say, I leave the porch light on when you're away.

Everyday love

Grocery List

Milk. Bread. Those crackers you like. Batteries for the remote you always lose. Flowers, because it's Tuesday. Nothing on the list says I love you and every line of it does.

Everyday love

Dishes

You wash, I dry. We never decided this. It just became the way. Twelve years of plates passed hand to hand, your sleeves pushed up, the radio low, the window going dark over the sink. If heaven is anything, I hope it's an hour like this, and a towel, and you.
No. 42

Everyday love

The Side of the Bed

You take the side by the window. I take the side by the door. Neither of us remembers deciding. Some nights I wake and you're turned toward me, breathing slow, one arm flung across my side like you're holding a place for me in a line that leads somewhere good.
No. 43

Everyday love

Rain Check

We cancelled the big dinner. You had a cold. I had a deadline. So we ate cereal on the floor and watched a movie we'd both seen, and you fell asleep on my shoulder twenty minutes in. I'd trade every fancy table in this city for that floor.
No. 44

Everyday love

Small Talk

How was your day? I ask, every day, and you tell me, every day — the coworker, the traffic, the strange bird by the office. None of it matters. All of it matters. I just want to know the shape of your hours so I can love them too.

After heartbreak, love again

Again

I said I'd never. I boarded up the windows and moved my heart to the back room, where it was safe and dark and quiet. Then you knocked — not on the door. On the wall, softly, from the outside, like you knew someone was in there. And I found I still remembered how to open things.

After heartbreak, love again

The Scar

You asked about the scar and I told you the true story, all of it, the parts I'm not proud of. You didn't flinch. You just said, "It healed, though." It did. And so, it turns out, did I.

After heartbreak, love again

Spring After a Long Winter

I had stopped expecting anything to grow. The ground was hard, the garden was a rumor. And then one morning — green. Small and stubborn and absolutely certain. That's how you came in. Not like a storm. Like a shoot through the frost, not asking permission, just reaching for the light like it's the most natural thing, and teaching me to reach for it too.

After heartbreak, love again

Second Chance

Nobody gets the first draft right. We cross out, we tear pages, we start over. I used to think that made me a failure at love. Now I think it made me a writer. And you — you're the poem I finally got right.

Open

Here's my heart, a little dented, patched in places, honest about it. It's not new. It's better than new. It's been through weather and come back. It knows what it's worth now, and it's choosing you.
