How to write a love haiku

By the A Poem About Love editors · Updated November 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Write a love poem freeGet published
Quick answer

A love haiku is a three-line poem, traditionally 5, 7 and 5 syllables, that captures a single moment with your love through one concrete image, often tied to a season. Show the moment instead of naming the feeling.

Haiku is a Japanese form built on attention. It doesn't explain or argue. It catches one moment and holds it still. That makes it a surprisingly good form for love, which mostly lives in moments.

The shape

  • Line 1: 5 syllables
  • Line 2: 7 syllables
  • Line 3: 5 syllables

Many modern English haiku poets treat 5-7-5 as a guideline rather than a law, since English syllables are longer than Japanese sound units. Short-long-short is the real goal.

Five steps

  1. Pick one moment. Not “our relationship.” One morning, one walk, one look.
  2. Find the season. Traditional haiku include a season word: snow, cherry blossom, cicadas, falling leaves.
  3. Use a concrete image. Muddy boots, a fogged window, two mugs.
  4. Add a “cut.” A turn or contrast between the first part and the last, often marked by a dash or line break.
  5. Don't say “love.” Let the image carry it.

Common mistakes

  • Cramming two moments into three lines
  • Abstract words like love, soul or forever doing the work an image should do
  • Counting syllables so hard the line stops sounding natural
