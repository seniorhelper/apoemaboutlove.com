What to write in a love poem for him

By the A Poem About Love editors · Updated November 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Write a love poem freeGet published
Quick answer

Write about what he does, not just how you feel: the way he fixes things, shows up, or makes you laugh. Name one specific memory, say thank you for something he thinks you didn't notice, and end with a plain promise.

On this page
  1. Seven things to write about
  2. An example, built from those ideas
  3. A simple structure that works
  4. What to avoid

A lot of men will tell you they're “not poem guys.” Then you'll find the card you gave them three years ago tucked in a glovebox. The trick to a love poem for him is to skip the flowers-and-moonlight language and write about the man he actually is.

Seven things to write about

  1. His hands. What do they fix, carry or hold? Hands are where a lot of men keep their love.
  2. The thing he does that nobody notices. Warming up your car. Checking the locks twice. Saving you the last bite.
  3. A moment you knew. Not the big date. The small moment, like the night the power went out.
  4. His steadiness. Many men want to be seen as someone you can lean on. Tell him he is.
  5. His laugh and what it does to a room.
  6. A thank you for something specific he doesn't think you saw.
  7. A simple promise for the future, said plainly.

An example, built from those ideas

You never mention that you scraped my windshield. I walk out to a clear car on the coldest mornings like the frost just changed its mind. I know it was you. I've always known it was you. That's the whole reason I'm not afraid of winter anymore.

Notice what it doesn't say: “you are my everything,” “my soulmate,” “my king.” Nothing wrong with those words, but they belong to everybody. The windshield belongs to the two of you.

A simple structure that works

Stanza one: something he does. Stanza two: what it means to you. Last line: one plain promise or thank you. Keep it to 8 to 12 lines. He'll read it twice if it's short enough to read once.

What to avoid

  • Comparing him to other men, even jokingly
  • Inside jokes so deep the poem only works if you explain it
  • Trying to sound like Shakespeare. Sound like you.

Questions

Many do, especially when the poem is specific and sincere rather than flowery. A short poem about something real he does tends to land best.

It doesn't have to. Free verse often sounds more natural and less greeting-card. If rhyme comes easily, use it; if you're forcing it, drop it.
