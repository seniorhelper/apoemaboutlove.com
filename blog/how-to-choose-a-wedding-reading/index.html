How to choose a wedding poem

By the A Poem About Love editors · Updated November 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Write a love poem freeGet published
Quick answer

Choose a wedding poem that takes under two minutes to read, sounds like the two of you, and is easy to say out loud. Read it to yourself at a slow pace first. If it takes longer than two minutes, or you stumble over the words, pick another.

A wedding reading has one job: to say something about love that the vows don't. It's a pause in the ceremony where everyone breathes. Here's how to pick one that fits.

Four quick rules

  1. Under two minutes. About 150 to 250 words read slowly.
  2. Easy on the tongue. Read it out loud. Tricky words and long clauses trip up nervous readers.
  3. Sounds like you. Formal couple, classic sonnet. Laid-back couple, something modern and plain.
  4. Check with your officiant if your ceremony is religious. Some venues have guidelines.

Classic choices that are free to use

These classics are in the public domain, so you can print them in your program without permission:

  • Shakespeare, Sonnet 116, the one that begins “Let me not to the marriage of true minds.” It's the most famous wedding sonnet in English.
  • Elizabeth Barrett Browning, “How Do I Love Thee?” (Sonnet 43). Warm, direct and beloved.
  • Anne Bradstreet, “To My Dear and Loving Husband.” Short, sincere and from the 1600s.
  • The Song of Songs, or the verses about love in 1 Corinthians 13, for religious ceremonies.
“Love is not love / Which alters when it alteration finds.”William Shakespeare, Sonnet 116 (public domain)

Modern and original options

Many modern wedding poems are still under copyright, which is fine for reading aloud at a private ceremony. Check before printing them in a program or posting them online. An original poem avoids the question entirely. Our wedding and vow poems were written for exactly this, and you're welcome to read one at your ceremony.

Who should read it?

Pick someone who loves you both and doesn't mind a microphone. Send them the poem at least two weeks early, printed in large type.

How to read it well

  • Read slower than feels natural. Nervous readers rush.
  • Pause at the end of each line or stanza, not just at periods.
  • Look up at the couple once or twice.
  • Print it. Don't read from a phone that might lock.

More on that in how to read a love poem out loud.

Questions

Under two minutes, which is about 150 to 250 words read at a slow, steady pace.

Reading a poem aloud at a private ceremony is generally fine. Printing it in programs or posting it online can require permission. Public-domain classics like Shakespeare's Sonnet 116 avoid the question. This is general information, not legal advice.
